Culture

Local culture & heritage

Communities who have lived alongside the forest for generations.

Folklore & stories

Legends of the forest

Elders pass down stories of the forest spirits believed to guard the rivers and old growth trees, teaching younger generations to move with respect and caution.

Tribal communities

People of the land

Soliga and Jenu Kuruba communities have coexisted with Bandipur's wildlife for centuries, holding deep traditional knowledge of the reserve's plants and animal behaviour.

Forest traditions

Living with nature

Seasonal foraging, medicinal plant use and rotational grazing practices reflect a working relationship with the forest that predates the reserve itself.

Festivals & customs

Festivals of Bandipur

Harvest festivals often include rituals thanking the forest, and mark the start of seasons when human-wildlife encounters tend to rise.

Previous conflicts

Human-wildlife conflict, 2024–26

A record of recent incidents around Bandipur, shared to help tourists understand why safety rules exist — not to alarm.

Recent incidents

DECEMBER 2023 · KUNDAKERE RANGE

A man's body was found inside the reserve, the third fatal tiger attack recorded in the park that month, prompting forest officials to step up patrols.

FEBRUARY 2025 · CHAMARAJANAGAR

A wild elephant chased two tourists who had approached it for a photograph. Both escaped unharmed, but the incident renewed warnings against close approaches for selfies.

2025 · BANDIPUR-WIDE

A spate of tiger attacks on the reserve's edge killed three people and left one person severely injured. The forest department captured 14 tigers in response, and jungle safaris were suspended for months amid farmer protests before being reopened in 2026.

AUGUST 2025 · BANDIPUR TIGER RESERVE

A tourist on foot along a park road was pinned down and injured by a wild tusker. Officials reiterated that visitors must stay inside their vehicles at all times.

Laws that apply

Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 — Section 51

General offences under the Act can draw up to 3 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to ₹1,00,000, or both.

Schedule I species & park offences

Harming a Schedule I animal (elephant, tiger and others) or hunting inside a national park or sanctuary carries a minimum of 3 years, extendable to 7 years' imprisonment, with a minimum fine of ₹10,000. Repeat offences carry a higher minimum fine.

Offences inside a tiger reserve

Hunting or tampering with a tiger reserve's boundaries carries a minimum of 7 years' imprisonment plus a fine ranging from ₹5 lakh to ₹30 lakh.

What this means for visitors

Leaving your vehicle, approaching, feeding or provoking any animal inside the reserve is treated as an offence under this Act — not just poaching.

Compensation & ex-gratia

Loss of life

Karnataka raised its ex-gratia payment for deaths from wildlife conflict from ₹15 lakh to ₹20 lakh from April 2025, alongside a ₹4,000 monthly pension to the family for five years.

Injury

Compensation is scaled to the severity of the injury, with higher payouts for critical or disabling injuries than for minor ones.

Crop, property & livestock loss

Claims are filed through the state's Parihara scheme, though a large backlog of crop-damage cases has built up in recent years due to funding delays.

Figures and cases are drawn from public news reporting for awareness purposes; confirm current rules and amounts with the Karnataka Forest Department before relying on them.

Activities

Enjoy Bandipur, safely

Guided, vehicle-based and forest department–run experiences that let you enjoy the reserve without direct contact with wildlife.

Bus & jeep safari

6:30–9:30 AM & 3:30–6:30 PM · Entry ~₹250

Forest department vehicles follow fixed game roads past salt licks and waterholes — the safest way to see elephants, gaur and deer up close.

Kabini backwaters boat safari

~2 hr 45 min · ~₹2,000 per head

A relaxed river cruise along the Kabini reservoir with sweeping views of the shoreline and resting waterbirds, with no need to leave the boat.

Forest department elephant camp

10–11 AM · ~₹50 per head

A short, supervised elephant ride run by the forest department — a calm way to get close to a resident elephant without entering the wild core zone.

Himavad Gopalaswamy Betta

8:30 AM–4 PM · Highest point, 1,450 m

A misty viewpoint and an old hilltop temple to Lord Krishna, reachable by permitted vehicle — go with a guide and stay on the marked path.

Guided birdwatching

Early morning · Along the Moyar & Kabini

Hornbills, eagles and waterbirds are easiest to spot on a slow morning walk with a naturalist, kept well clear of grazing wildlife.

Day trips just outside the reserve

Ooty · BR Hills · Sultan Bathery

Ooty hill station (~40 km), the BR Hills temple, and Sultan Bathery town all make relaxed additions to a trip, well away from the core forest.

Timings, fees and routes change seasonally — always confirm at the official Bandipur safari counter before booking.
